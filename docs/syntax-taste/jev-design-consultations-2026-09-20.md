# Jev design consultations and engineering judgments

20 September 2026. Advisory evidence accompanying the [technical specification](technical-spec.md). The complete eight-topic exercise has been redone with three fresh live requests, each using a different full presentation of the same decision problem. All eight engineering conclusions are retained after reassessment. The redo changes the consultation evidence and its documentation; it introduces no new syntax or runtime contract.

## Two distinct experiments

**Experiment 1 is superseded as the required paraphrasing exercise.** It submitted eight topics in three live requests but changed only the question instructions. The structured context and candidate descriptions were identical. That narrower method did not satisfy the user's requirement to reword everything: context, questions, and option definitions. Its 24 judgments do not count toward the redo and are not pooled with it as votes. The ten original evidence files, including requests, responses, validation, and native probes, remain byte-for-byte unchanged; [recorded hashes](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/prior-evidence-sha256.json) and [redo validation](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/validation.json) establish preservation. The [verbatim prior report](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/previous-report.txt) records its original results and engineering judgments. Its relative links reflect its former location.

**Experiment 2 is the completed full-presentation redo.** Three agents independently drafted presentations of all eight original decision problems, then reviewed one another's drafts against a [canonical problem ledger](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/problem-ledger.json). Every round rewrites all 54 natural-language fields: purpose, shared constraints, evidence limits, snapshot explanation, topic facts, question instructions, and all 29 candidate descriptions. Semantic topic/option keys, option order, exact technical identifiers, code and numeric facts are preserved. This is a controlled retrospective replay of the original tradeoffs; every request says so explicitly rather than representing the subsequently revised specifications as the old baseline. No previous Jev response or later engineering selection was supplied to the model.

The [TypeSafe skill](../../.agents/skills/typesafe-ai/SKILL.md) was followed, including a [fresh read of the live index, API, Choice, state, confidence and self-consistency documentation](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/source-refresh.json). Jev received the required facts and alternatives in its state. It performed no research and returned typed choices, distributions, and confidence values, not prose explanations.

There is no categorical disagreement in the fresh sample. The greatest probability movement is in aggregate typing (0.82–0.98) and resource deadlines (0.88–0.98). In fresh round 2, mandatory contextual aggregate typing receives 0.13 and `review_needed` 0.05; drain without host policy receives 0.08 and `review_needed` 0.04. Those alternatives therefore received explicit post-result review. A fallback that never exposes its failures still needs no source-visible nominal payload type; any observation or forwarding does. A close deadline still cannot guarantee host termination while uncancelled work holds a lease. Neither alternative improves those requirements without changing a deliberate tradeoff. The retained choices follow those arguments, not a vote.

The model gives no rationale identifying which rewritten phrase caused a probability change. This design varies whole presentations without repeated samples of each presentation, so it cannot separate wording sensitivity from ordinary model variability or attribute a change to context versus option wording. Fixed option order, related problems, the same underlying model, and shared evidence make these correlated judgments. There is no calibrated acceptance threshold, independent replication claim, pooled majority rule, or assumption that a 1.00 reported probability proves correctness.

## Reassessment of all eight conclusions

The following reasons are engineering judgments, not explanations returned by Jev. Each topic was reconsidered before and after the fresh results. The [coordination review](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/postflight-coordination-review.md) and [capability review](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/postflight-capabilities-review.md) record separate post-result scrutiny. **No conclusion changed from the reconciled specification preceding this redo.** The earlier technical changes described below remain adopted; they were not newly introduced by these three calls.

1. **Exact integer JSON: retain A6/A10's exact integral-token rule.** JSON Schema integer acceptance follows mathematical integrality rather than decimal-point spelling. Accepting `1` but rejecting exact `1.0` or `1e0` would create a provider compatibility failure without protecting additional precision. Source-token recovery, bounded coefficient/decimal-shift normalization, and native BigInt preserve exactness without source int/float coercion. Trailing-zero checks, lexical bounds for enormous exponents, and a cumulative canonical-integer byte budget prevent unbounded expansion. Fractional and quoted numbers still fail. Encoder preflight independently guards bigints before decimal formatting and bounds escaping/structure before native JSON.stringify. The strict-lexeme alternative remains simpler but retains the stated interoperability cost.

2. **Generation shapes: retain A10's bounded initial subset.** The required generation-to-judgment traces fit nonrecursive records and arrays. A shared codec plus an explicit provider-admissibility predicate preserves reuse and rejects broader graphs before sending. Adding variant/reference schema emission now creates obligations without closing a required trace. This is an intentional initial capability boundary, not a claim that the provider cannot support recursion. The broader graph alternative remains coherent if future required programs justify it.

3. **Aggregate typing: retain Q6's lazy expected named type.** An unobserved internal aggregate requires only the finite private participant set. Observation or forwarding requires one unique expected named variant covering that set, so no untyped aggregate payload escapes. Requiring a nominal type even for an ignoring fallback creates unused declarations; searching unrelated declarations for a unique cover makes inference depend on package contents. Round 2's greater weight on mandatory contextual typing does not expose a missing escape rule in the retained contract.

4. **Resource deadlines: retain Q10/P6/P10/P12's external-supervisor boundary.** A close timeout reports a failure while a live lease may prevent cleanup forever. A recorded unsuccessful status is not an exit guarantee. Owners remain responsible for outstanding operations and leases are not invalidated. A deployment requiring bounded host termination must supply an external supervisor; process killing provides no Can cancellation, clean-drain, or remote-rollback guarantee. Drain without an explicit host policy would leave that operational requirement unresolved; an internal hard stop would introduce a new runtime policy. The fresh probability movement does not alter this lifecycle argument.

5. **Sequential map: retain C7/Q11's native `Array.fromAsync` lowering.** Its sequential awaited callback execution and first-failure stopping match the chosen contract; the earlier published-algorithm review and [Bun probes](evidence/jev-design-2026-09-20/native-probes.json) support those properties. Native index iteration protects input records from thenable assimilation; callbacks return private completion boxes and a synchronous native map extracts payloads afterward. This adapter preserves legal callable `then` fields on both sides. A predecessor graph or handwritten traversal adds scheduling or traversal work without a demonstrated contract benefit. Compiler-target conformance remains required.

6. **Fixture queues: retain P3's canonical shared FIFO with its stated limit.** Successive identical calls need an ordered sequence of supplied outcomes. A shared FIFO supports that sequence within the approved grammar. Argument search can hide ordering mistakes, while independent participant queues change one shared sequence into multiple scripts. The contract must still name the canonical test scheduler and its queue identity precisely. This observes one controlled schedule; it is not proof about all native interleavings. Separate controlled-promise conformance must exercise early settlements and surviving losers. Higher selected probabilities in the redo do not remove that boundary.

7. **Generic checking: retain C4's concrete specialization.** Without approved bound syntax, parametric-only checking excludes useful generic operations or requires concrete wrappers. Checking every branch of each reachable concrete instance permits those operations while retaining declaration-time grammar/name checks, explicit use-site diagnostics, and rejection of expanding specialization. Assertion examples do not prove correctness for arbitrary types. Some errors appearing at use sites are a deliberate cost of the retained expressiveness, not a result erased by repeated agreement.

8. **Promise payloads: retain universal private completion boxes in C4/Q3/C7.** Callable `then` is legal record data, and native promise resolution can invoke it. The recorded Bun observation demonstrates the assimilation hazard and preserved identity when protected. Every native promise boundary therefore carries a private null-prototype nonthenable box; synchronous generated code alone extracts the Can payload. This includes ordinary calls, callbacks, accumulators, and coordination results. A source-name ban would restrict otherwise legal data. Mangling record fields would impose a representation rule without replacing the need to reason about every native promise boundary. The retained contract permits ordinary generated record layouts while protecting promise crossings.

## Sources, limits, and remaining blockers

The [TypeSafe API](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api), [Choice guide](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/choice), [state guide](https://docs.typesafe.ai/concepts/state), and [confidence guide](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence) define the advisory request/answer model. The [self-consistency cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/consistency_choice_cookbook) supplies a comparison method; its demonstration thresholds were not adopted as language-design policy.

The numerical interoperability fact comes from the [JSON Schema numeric-type reference](https://json-schema.org/understanding-json-schema/reference/numeric). Native lowering facts come from the [TC39 Array.fromAsync algorithm](https://tc39.es/proposal-array-from-async/) and [ECMAScript Promise resolution](https://tc39.es/ecma262/multipage/control-abstraction-objects.html#sec-promise-resolve-functions), supplemented by the preserved native probes. Provider generation breadth is documented by [OpenAI structured outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs).

An [independent final report audit](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/final-report-review.json) found no material mismatch with the raw evidence. [Documentation verification](evidence/jev-design-full-redo-2026-09-20/final-validation.json) checks local links, JSON, preserved hashes, and whitespace.

No blocker remains for this bounded redo: all eight topics received the required three fresh full-presentation judgments, all response contracts passed validation, and every conclusion was reassessed. No additional source syntax or user policy decision was needed. The consultation reference and specification evidence statement now distinguish the two experiments and require variation of the entire presentation. The historical audit, approved syntax, runtime contracts, original evidence, and implementation files remain unchanged by this redo.

This is documentation and advisory evidence only. The live calls did not run generated Can code, validate a Can provider adapter, assess an application model's quality, establish calibration, or exercise a live database or deployment. The existing Bun probes were reused as bounded native observations and were not rerun or promoted into compiler conformance. Parser/checker, lowering, provider-fixture, scheduler, database-driver, and HTMX integration verification remain implementation obligations. Those limits remain even when all three presentations select the same candidate.
