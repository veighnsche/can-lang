## Actionable design findings

1. **P1 — Browser launch isolation must be a named admission and receipt boundary.** The [lifecycle](../../native-can-test-lifecycle-2026-09-30.md) lines 90–114 and 174–190 require sealed intervals and resource release, while [capabilities](../../native-can-test-capabilities-2026-09-30.md) lines 122–123 and 143–157 focus on process/context/event facts. The repaired PM-B1 route and process/scratch cleanup were accepted in the [second finalization](../native-can-test-preparatory-checks-2026-09-30/run-20260930T163537-e5ff9c-finalization.json) lines 10–17, yet a later user report established an unexpected Keychain dialog; see [results](../../native-can-test-preparatory-results-2026-09-30.md) lines 20–26 and [summary](../native-can-test-preparatory-checks-2026-09-30/execution-summary.json) lines 38–67. The exact dialog process was not captured. Add an explicit browser admission profile for noninteractive, test-only credential behavior, identified process/profile provenance and a qualified host-UI observation scope. An unexplained native modal or credential-store interaction fails browser isolation and blocks further launch under that profile. Flags are candidate configuration, not proof of absence. Do not touch personal Keychain settings or automatically dismiss dialogs. Bind the observation scope and its limits into N's receipt so clean process/scratch release cannot be mistaken for all host effects being clean.

2. **P1 — Specify how a credible late effect invalidates an already published clean result.** [Lifecycle](../../native-can-test-lifecycle-2026-09-30.md) lines 101–119 binds the Can report and N receipt and rejects missing or conflicting records, but it does not say how the consumer handles a later fact outside the originally observed scope. The PM-B1 [second finalization](../native-can-test-preparatory-checks-2026-09-30/run-20260930T163537-e5ff9c-finalization.json) lines 10–17 said `mechanism_supported`; the later [canonical summary](../native-can-test-preparatory-checks-2026-09-30/execution-summary.json) lines 38–53 correctly superseded only that acceptance, without rewriting the historical receipt. Specify an append-only correction/invalidation record keyed to the prior run/report/receipt identity, preserve the old observation and its narrower scope, and make consumers consult the current correction chain before qualification. This is a protocol/design rule, not a demand for another browser run.

3. **P2 — Keep input settlement separate from response delivery during abort and close.** [Lifecycle](../../native-can-test-lifecycle-2026-09-30.md) lines 174–188 says to abort paused routes and drain callbacks, and [capabilities](../../native-can-test-capabilities-2026-09-30.md) lines 128–129, 139–145 already provide separate logical operations. Make their terminal contract explicit: the real input action can fulfill after its route was aborted; fulfillment alone proves neither delivery nor a successful DOM result. The first PM-B1 [raw record](../native-can-test-preparatory-checks-2026-09-30/run-20260930T162840-599df6-results.json) lines 1320–1368 shows `closing → route_aborted → input_settled(fulfilled) → observation_error → closed`; [results](../../native-can-test-preparatory-results-2026-09-30.md) lines 18–20 explain the instrument error and the repaired `closing < route_aborted < input_settled < closed` control. Require independent route/delivery outcome, response/DOM witness for positive delivery, phase-gated post-settlement reads, and a final callback/error seal. In a deliberate abort control, either actual click terminal outcome may be valid if recorded; an attempted marker read after closing must not create or conceal an oracle result.

4. **P2 — Make source-era statements time-scoped after preparatory execution.** [Lifecycle](../../native-can-test-lifecycle-2026-09-30.md) lines 17–21 says the bounded hard-case register is unrun and line 8 says no tests ran “in this round.” That was true when authored, but PM-N1/N2/F1/B1 mechanics now have results. Keep the lifecycle design status and implementation/qualification gate, while linking the [current preparatory results](../../native-can-test-preparatory-results-2026-09-30.md) and saying those are scoped mechanics, not the integrated B1/N1/N2/F1 experiments. This prevents a reader from treating the executed prep receipts as nonexistent or as integrated coverage.

A PID, path sighting, exited leader or process-group signal is not descendant containment or deletion authority. The PM-B1 parent proved cleanup of its fixed observed graph only ([browser scope](../native-can-test-preparatory-checks-2026-09-30/browser-scope.md) lines 25–29); it did not qualify arbitrary detached descendants or N. Admission needs demonstrable host-specific authority and an inactivity proof for each claimed graph, otherwise the affected variant remains blocked. The current architecture already states this as an implementation/qualification gate.
- **Descriptor EOF and non-reader exit.** [Capabilities](../../native-can-test-capabilities-2026-09-30.md) lines 93–112 and [hard cases](../../native-can-test-hard-cases-2026-09-30.md) lines 81–90 distinguish accepted pipe bytes, EOF, import readiness and child exit. PM-F1's withheld-EOF control stayed failed despite later exit, and its non-reader wrote only 65,536 of 1,048,576 offered bytes yet stopped the writer ([results](../../native-can-test-preparatory-results-2026-09-30.md) line 11; [descriptor scope](../native-can-test-preparatory-checks-2026-09-30/descriptor-scope.md) lines 24–41). The Python/POSIX-to-Bun result does not qualify Go `ExtraFiles`, real launcher sanitization, fd 4 lease or generated Can access. Retain those F1/F2 implementation gates; do not add a second EOF success rule based on child exit.
- **Malformed/partial reports and cleanup finalization.** [Lifecycle](../../native-can-test-lifecycle-2026-09-30.md) lines 36–59, 82–127 and 140–162 already classify early `ok`, unknown/duplicate checks, gaps, wrong identity, stale/missing report and missing matching N receipt as non-success. That is consistent with provisional-to-receipted accounting. Negative controls for malformed aggregate, worker/controller death and receipt loss remain unrun qualification work; no design rewrite is warranted beyond the late-effect correction in finding 2.
- **Sampled resource maxima versus strict containment.** [Lifecycle](../../native-can-test-lifecycle-2026-09-30.md) lines 257–293 explicitly calls its limits policy ceilings, requires finite type-specific values and blocks a profile lacking qualified strict disk/memory/process enforcement. [Results](../../native-can-test-preparatory-results-2026-09-30.md) lines 32–42 labels its process/RSS/scratch peaks sampled observations, not strict guarantees. PM-B1's 1.66–1.72 GiB RSS and 15 observed processes are feasibility facts only. Do not promote the Python T fixture or these samples into N's host containment receipt; qualify actual enforcement or block strict-profile admission.
